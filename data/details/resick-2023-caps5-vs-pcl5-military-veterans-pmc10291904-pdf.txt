This study investigates why self-report questionnaires consistently yield higher PTSD scores than clinician-administered interviews in military and veteran populations. Researchers analyzed data from four clinical trials involving active duty military personnel and veterans to compare baseline and post-treatment assessments. The main finding is that participants using the self-report scale were more likely to select extreme response options than interviewers, suggesting that differences in how questions are worded and understood drive the scoring discrepancy.
