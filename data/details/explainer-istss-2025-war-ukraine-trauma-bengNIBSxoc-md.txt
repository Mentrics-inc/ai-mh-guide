The speakers address the unique challenges faced by those still in Ukraine and the millions who have fled as refugees. They emphasize that trauma support must evolve from standard clinical treatments to immediate, practical aid suited for ongoing danger and displacement. **Why This Matters** The scale of displacement is unprecedented, with millions facing uncertainty, separation from family, and exposure to violence. Standard mental health models often fail in active conflict zones because they assume safety and stability. Experts highlight that immediate needs—such as food, shelter, and information—are foundational to mental well-being. Furthermore, the crisis reveals gaps in supporting vulnerable groups, including children, the elderly, and those with pre-existing severe mental health conditions.
