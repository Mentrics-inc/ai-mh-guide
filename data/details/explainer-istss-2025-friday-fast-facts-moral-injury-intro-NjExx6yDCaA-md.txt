<!-- explainer from istss-2025-friday-fast-facts-moral-injury-intro-transcript-yt-NjExx6yDCaA.txt · 4s --> # Understanding Moral Injury Moral injury is a psychological response that occurs when a person witnesses, fails to prevent, or participates in an event that violates their deeply held moral or ethical beliefs. Unlike general trauma, which often involves fear for one’s safety, moral injury centers on the conflict between actions and personal values. This conflict can lead to significant emotional, spiritual, and social distress, making daily life difficult. It is not limited to military personnel; growing recognition shows that people from all walks of life can experience moral injury when their core values are challenged by external events. # Why It Matters Recognizing moral injury is crucial because it helps individuals understand that their distress may stem from a values-based conflict rather than just fear or anxiety. This distinction is vital for effective support and healing.
