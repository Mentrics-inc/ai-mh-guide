While adult suicide rates remain relatively stable, rates of suicide and self-harm among young people are increasing significantly, particularly among females, First Nations youth, and those in rural areas. The speakers emphasize that behind every statistic is a distressed young person, urging a shift from viewing self-harm as "attention-seeking" to recognizing it as a serious cry for help that often escalates without proper care. **Why This Matters** Traditional suicide prevention methods have largely failed to engage young people effectively. Historically, research and interventions were designed by adults for adults, leading to a disconnect with youth needs. Furthermore, while young people spend hours daily on social media, this space was previously viewed only as a risk factor for "contagion" or harmful behavior.
