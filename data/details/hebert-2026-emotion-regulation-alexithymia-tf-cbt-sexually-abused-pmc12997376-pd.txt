This study examines how changes in emotional regulation and the ability to identify feelings contribute to the effectiveness of trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy for children who have experienced sexual abuse. The researchers analyzed data from 234 children treated at a child advocacy center, using parent and clinician reports to measure symptom changes before and after the intervention. The main finding is that improved emotional regulation significantly explains the reduction in trauma-related symptoms across various areas, while difficulties in identifying feelings specifically helped reduce dissociation and internalizing problems.
