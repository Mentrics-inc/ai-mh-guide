<!-- explainer from istss-2025-rauch-ptsd-innovators-transcript-yt-Q-tqNXrdhiQ.txt · 9s --> **What the Content Covers** This interview features Dr. Sheila Rous discussing Prolonged Exposure (PE), a highly effective psychological treatment for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD). Dr. Rous explains the three core components of PE: psychoeducation to understand trauma responses, imaginal exposure to process traumatic memories safely, and in vivo exposure to gradually face avoided real-life situations. The discussion highlights that PE is adaptable, working across various cultures, settings (including primary care and telehealth), and for patients with complex co-occurring conditions like substance use or personality disorders. It also touches on recent innovations, such as shorter session formats and methods to help slow-responding patients improve their outcomes. **Why It Matters** PTSD is fundamentally a disorder of avoidance, meaning many sufferers never seek help. By demonstrating that PE can be delivered in diverse, accessible formats—such as brief sessions in primary care or via telehealth—healthcare providers can overcome barriers to entry.
