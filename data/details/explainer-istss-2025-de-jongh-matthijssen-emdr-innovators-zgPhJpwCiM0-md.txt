<!-- explainer from istss-2025-de-jongh-matthijssen-emdr-innovators-transcript-yt-zgPhJpwCiM0.txt · 9s --> **What EMDR Therapy Is and How It Works** Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) is a psychological treatment recommended for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and other trauma-related conditions. While originally thought to work through bilateral eye movements, current research suggests the therapy relies on "working memory" mechanisms. By recalling a traumatic memory while performing a distracting task, such as following a therapist’s moving fingers, the brain processes the memory differently. This dual attention reduces the vividness and emotional intensity of the memory, allowing it to be reconsolidated with less distress. The discussion also highlights "EMDR 2.0," an updated approach that uses various distracting tasks to enhance this effect. **Why This Matters for Mental Health Care** Understanding the science behind EMDR helps clinicians treat a wide range of patients, including children, those with intellectual disabilities, and individuals with co-occurring conditions like psychosis or depression.
