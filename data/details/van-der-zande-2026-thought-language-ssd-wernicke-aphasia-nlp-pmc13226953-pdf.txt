The study compares the speech patterns of patients with schizophrenia spectrum disorders and Wernicke’s aphasia to determine if automated language analysis can distinguish between these two conditions. Researchers used machine learning algorithms and large language models to analyze linguistic features such as syntax and word meaning in spontaneous speech transcripts. The results showed that the language models could accurately identify Wernicke’s aphasia based on specific linguistic errors, but struggled to distinguish schizophrenia from healthy controls, suggesting that thought disorders do not always produce clear surface-level language defects.
