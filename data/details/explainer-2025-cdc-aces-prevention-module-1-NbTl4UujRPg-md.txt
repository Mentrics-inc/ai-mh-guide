<!-- explainer from 2025-cdc-aces-prevention-module-1-transcript-yt-NbTl4UujRPg.txt · 3s --> # Understanding Adverse Childhood Experiences This training introduces the concept of Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs), which include events like child abuse, neglect, and other household challenges. Through the personal story of a student named Eddie, the content illustrates how family instability—such as a parent’s severe illness—can disrupt a child’s life. However, it also highlights how timely support from caring adults, such as school counselors and social workers, can help families recover and thrive. The material explains that while ACEs are common across all backgrounds, they are not inevitable and can be prevented or mitigated through community intervention. # Why This Matters ACEs are significant because their impact extends far beyond childhood. Research shows that these early stressors can lead to long-term physical and mental health problems, lower academic achievement, and risky behaviors in adulthood. Furthermore, the effects can span generations, as children whose parents experienced ACEs are at higher risk of facing similar challenges. Understanding this link is crucial
