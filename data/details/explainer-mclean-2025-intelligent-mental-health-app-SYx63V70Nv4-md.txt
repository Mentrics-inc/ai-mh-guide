<!-- explainer from mclean-2025-intelligent-mental-health-app-transcript-yt-SYx63V70Nv4.txt · 21s --> **What This Content Covers** Researchers from Dartmouth University are developing "intelligent" mental health apps that move beyond generic, one-size-fits-all messages. The presentation outlines a system that uses smartphone and wearable sensors to passively monitor user behavior, such as sleep, movement, and social interaction. Instead of relying on rigid rules (like "send a notification if steps are low"), the app uses machine learning and large language models to interpret this data in context. For example, it can distinguish between a student studying late (where a walk prompt would be annoying) and someone sitting alone in a cafeteria (where a supportive prompt might help). The goal is to deliver personalized support at the precise moment it is needed. **Why It Matters** Digital mental health tools often fail because users abandon them quickly. In real-world settings, up to 97% of users drop off within 30 days, largely because interventions feel irrelevant or mistimed.
