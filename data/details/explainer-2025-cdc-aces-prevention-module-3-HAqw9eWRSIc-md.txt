It explains how early adversity can limit opportunities for education, employment, and income. The core of this understanding comes from the CDC-Kaiser Permanente Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study. Conducted between 1995 and 1997 in Southern California, this large-scale investigation surveyed over 17,000 adults. Participants completed confidential surveys about their childhoods and underwent physical exams to assess their current health. The study identified ten specific adverse experiences, categorized into three groups: abuse, neglect, and household challenges. # Why This Matters Recognizing the link between early trauma and adult health is crucial for public health and social support systems.
