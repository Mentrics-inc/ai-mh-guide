IntroductionArtificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly incorporated into mental healthcare, offering opportunities to improve diagnostic accuracy, service accessibility, and administrative efficiency. However, effective implementation depends on the mental health workforce's awareness, perceptions, and ethical concerns related to AI. Evidence regarding these factors remains limited within diverse practice settings in the United States (US).MethodsA descriptive cross-sectional survey was conducted among mental health professionals and trainees practicing in two US states: California and New Jersey. Data were collected using a structured, self-administered online questionnaire adapted from the validated Shinners Artificial Intelligence Perception (SHAIP) scale and expanded to include an ethical concern domain. The survey assessed demographic characteristics, AI awareness, perceptions, ethical concerns, and barriers and facilitators to AI adoption.
