<!-- explainer from istss-2025-sleep-trauma-ptsd-transcript-yt-X4x9hEvsDrs.txt · 5s --> # Understanding Sleep, Trauma, and PTSD This presentation by clinical psychologist Anthony Refsy highlights the critical, often overlooked connection between sleep disturbances and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). While the discussion focuses on PTSD, it emphasizes that sleep issues are "trans-diagnostic," meaning they significantly impact recovery from various trauma-related conditions, including depression, substance use, and suicidal behavior. Refsy shares his clinical experience with veterans, noting that poor sleep severely hampers their ability to engage in trauma-focused therapies like prolonged exposure. When patients are exhausted, irritable, or anxious, they struggle to process difficult memories effectively. # Why This Matters Sleep is not merely a passive state; it is an active component of trauma resilience and recovery. Chronic sleep problems act as barriers to treatment, making it harder for individuals to benefit from therapy.
