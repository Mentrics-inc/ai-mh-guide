This paper reviews the current state of artificial intelligence in psychiatry, focusing on its clinical applications alongside significant methodological and ethical limitations. The analysis is based on a narrative review of existing literature, including systematic reviews and empirical studies on machine learning models for diagnosis and risk prediction. The main finding is that while AI shows technical promise in controlled research settings, its real-world clinical utility is limited by bias and lack of validation, raising urgent concerns about data governance and the pathologization of human diversity.
