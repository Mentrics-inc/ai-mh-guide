It highlights that while millions are forcibly displaced by conflict and disaster, current trauma models often fail to capture the unique, prolonged, and complex nature of refugee experiences. The speaker, Dr. Belinda Liddell, emphasizes that refugees face not only past atrocities but also ongoing stressors like family separation, fear for loved ones, and the difficulties of resettling in a new country. # Why This Matters Understanding these specific dynamics is crucial because standard trauma treatments may not be effective for this population. Refugees endure multiple, long-term traumatic events before, during, and after migration. Ignoring these distinct factors can lead to inadequate support.
