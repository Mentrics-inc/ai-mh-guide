Rather than focusing solely on treating problems after they occur, this framework emphasizes prevention. Communities are increasingly using data on Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)—such as abuse, neglect, or household dysfunction—to guide decision-making. By understanding the prevalence and impact of these experiences, policymakers and community partners can create environments that protect children from harm before it starts. # Why This Matters Preventing adverse childhood experiences is crucial because early trauma can have long-lasting effects on physical and mental health. When communities proactively address the root causes of these experiences, they reduce the burden on healthcare and social services later in life.
