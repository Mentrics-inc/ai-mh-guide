ObjectiveTo examine whether generative artificial intelligence-assisted visual art therapy improves cognitive function and alleviates anxiety and depression in community-dwelling older adults with mild cognitive impairment.MethodsA quasi-experimental, cluster-gated design was employed in Shengli Street Community, Enshi City (September 2025-April 2026). Eighty eligible older adults were enrolled and assigned by administrative grid to an intervention group (<i>n</i> = 40) or a control group (<i>n</i> = 40). The control group continued routine community activities; the intervention group additionally received a 12-week generative artificial intelligence-assisted visual art therapy program. The primary outcome was change in Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) total score; the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) served as the secondary outcome. Assessments occurred at baseline, 1 week post-intervention, and at three-month follow-up. Cohen's d effect sizes are reported.ResultsSeventy-two participants completed the study.
