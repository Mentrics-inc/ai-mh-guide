This paper introduces the Contextual Resonance Theory of Psychopathology, which argues that trauma persists across generations through the reproduction of harmful social and environmental patterns rather than solely through biological inheritance. The authors develop this theoretical framework by integrating concepts from cognitive neuroscience, systems theory, and ecological psychology to explain how trauma-shaped environments recursively transmit vulnerability. The main finding is that psychopathology emerges from ongoing interactions between individuals and these pathogenic contexts, and that this cycle can be reversed through corrective recontextualization in safe and predictable environments.
