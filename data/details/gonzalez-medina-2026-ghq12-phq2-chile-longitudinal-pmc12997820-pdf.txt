BACKGROUND: Brief questionnaires have been applied in poor urban populations to monitor their mental health. Mental health outcomes encompass diverse symptoms that differ in cause and functional impact; however, most studies combine these symptoms as if they represented a single construct. Longitudinal validation of health instruments requires understanding the psychometric properties and causal structures between measurement points to distinguish measurement error from true change before interpreting intervention effectiveness. However, the longitudinal psychometric properties of the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ-12) and Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-2) in Latin American social housing populations remain unknown. This study assessed the dimensionality and longitudinal psychometric properties of these instruments in Chilean populations targeted by urban regeneration interventions.
