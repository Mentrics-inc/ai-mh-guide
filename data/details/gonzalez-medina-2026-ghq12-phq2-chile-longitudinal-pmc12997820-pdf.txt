This study evaluates the reliability and structure of two common mental health questionnaires among low-income residents in Chilean urban neighborhoods. The researchers analyzed data from 955 participants surveyed at two time points six months apart to test the instruments' consistency and validity. They found that while the questionnaires are generally reliable, people’s changing perceptions of their symptoms over time can distort the results, making it difficult to tell if improvements are real or just due to shifting personal standards.
