<b>Background:</b> No controlled studies incorporating randomization have been conducted to investigate the effectiveness of online eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) treatment, despite its use in clinical practice.<b>Objective:</b> This study evaluated the effect of online EMDR treatment in adults aged 18-65 years with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) resulting from multiple traumas.<b>Method:</b> A multiple baseline single-case experimental design (<i>n</i> = 21) was employed. Participants were patients with PTSD due to multiple traumas, recruited from a mental healthcare institution in the Netherlands. They were randomly assigned to baseline phases of 2, 3.5, or 5.5 weeks. After this, participants received 10 weekly online EMDR sessions. The primary outcome was the total score on an adapted version of the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5), which was administered twice a week during baseline and intervention phases, and once 12 weeks after the end of the intervention phase.
