<!-- explainer from 2025-cdc-aces-prevention-module-5-transcript-yt-vezTnx8s2j8.txt · 3s --> # Understanding Adverse Childhood Experiences This lesson explores the well-documented link between Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) and various negative outcomes in adulthood. Based on data from the original CDC-Kaiser Permanente study and other sources, the content highlights how early life stress can shape long-term health and well-being. ### Why It Matters ACEs are far more common than many people realize. Research shows that nearly two-thirds of adults have experienced at least one ACE, and more than one in five have experienced three or more. These experiences are widespread across different demographic groups, including variations in sex, race, ethnicity, education, and income. Understanding this prevalence is crucial because it helps society recognize that trauma is a shared public health issue, not just an individual problem. ### Key Takeaways 1. **ACEs Are Common Across Groups** Adverse experiences do not belong to one specific demographic.
