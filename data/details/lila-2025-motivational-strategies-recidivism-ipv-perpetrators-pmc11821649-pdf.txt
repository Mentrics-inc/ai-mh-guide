This study examines whether adding motivational strategies to standard treatment programs helps men who commit intimate partner violence and have substance use problems stay in therapy and avoid reoffending. Researchers compared 716 participants, half of whom received the standard intervention and half of whom received the standard intervention plus an individualized motivational plan, tracking their dropout rates and recidivism. The main finding is that while men with substance use problems typically have worse outcomes, the motivational strategies successfully eliminated these disparities, allowing them to perform as well as those without substance issues.
